# Punjabi Sikh wedding (Anand Karaj)

The Sikh marriage ceremony, Anand Karaj ("ceremony of bliss"), takes place in the presence of the Guru Granth Sahib, usually at a gurdwara in the morning. Sikh tradition does not require an astrologically chosen date or time; many families simply pick a convenient day. Customs around the ceremony, such as Jaggo and Choora, are Punjabi family traditions and vary between families. Check details with the gurdwara's granthi and your elders. Durations below are typical planning ranges.

## Kurmai

- **Key:** kurmai
- **When:** Weeks or months before the wedding, often at the gurdwara or the groom's home
- **Typical duration:** 90 minutes
- **Participants:** Groom, both families, granthi (for the Ardas)
- **Items:** Kara (steel bangle), dried fruit (chhuare), sweets, gifts from the bride's family to the groom
- **Significance:** The engagement: after an Ardas (prayer), the bride's family blesses the groom and the families commit to the marriage.

## Chunni Chadana

- **Key:** chunni_chadana
- **When:** After the Kurmai, sometimes on the same day
- **Typical duration:** 45 minutes
- **Participants:** Bride, groom's mother and female relatives
- **Items:** Red or embroidered chunni (headscarf), jewellery, sweets
- **Significance:** The groom's family places a chunni over the bride's head, welcoming her into their family.

## Maiyan and Vatna

- **Key:** maiyan
- **When:** One to three days before the wedding; separately at the bride's and the groom's homes
- **Typical duration:** 60 minutes
- **Participants:** Bride or groom, female relatives, close friends
- **Items:** Vatna paste (turmeric, gram flour, mustard oil), a low wooden seat, red cloth held overhead, folk songs
- **Significance:** Relatives apply a turmeric paste to cleanse and brighten the bride and groom before the wedding.

## Jaggo

- **Key:** jaggo
- **When:** Night before the wedding
- **Typical duration:** 120 minutes
- **Participants:** Families and friends, led by the maternal aunt (maami) carrying the jaggo
- **Items:** Decorated brass or clay pot (jaggo) with oil lamps, sticks with bells, dhol player
- **Significance:** Relatives dance and sing through the night with a lamp-lit pot, announcing the wedding to the neighbourhood.

## Choora

- **Key:** choora
- **When:** Early morning of the wedding day
- **Typical duration:** 45 minutes
- **Participants:** Bride, bride's maternal uncle (mama) and family
- **Items:** Choora (set of red and ivory bangles), bowl of milk and rose petals, kaleere (gold or silver ornaments tied to the bangles)
- **Significance:** The bride's maternal uncle gives her the choora bangles; relatives tie on kaleere as blessings.

## Sehrabandi and Baraat

- **Key:** sehrabandi_baraat
- **When:** Morning of the wedding day, before leaving for the gurdwara
- **Typical duration:** 60 minutes
- **Participants:** Groom, groom's family and friends, his sister or sister-in-law
- **Items:** Sehra (veil of flowers or pearls) tied over the turban, kalgi (turban ornament), dhol player, transport for the baraat
- **Significance:** The groom's turban is adorned with a sehra, and the wedding party sets off for the bride's side.

## Milni

- **Key:** milni
- **When:** On the baraat's arrival at the gurdwara or venue, after a short Ardas
- **Typical duration:** 30 minutes
- **Participants:** Male relatives of both families in matching pairs (fathers, uncles, brothers), granthi or raagi
- **Items:** Garlands, small gifts or envelopes, blankets or shawls (in some families)
- **Significance:** Relatives of the same relationship from each family embrace and exchange garlands, joining the two families.

## Anand Karaj

- **Key:** anand_karaj
- **When:** Morning, in the gurdwara's darbar hall, after breakfast for guests
- **Typical duration:** 90 minutes
- **Participants:** Bride, groom, bride's father, granthi, raagis (hymn singers), sangat (congregation)
- **Items:** Palla (long scarf worn by the groom), head coverings for all guests, Guru Granth Sahib, Karah Prashad
- **Significance:** The couple walk around the Guru Granth Sahib four times as the four Laavan are sung, each round a stage of married life.

## Laavan

- **Key:** laavan
- **When:** Within the Anand Karaj, after the bride's father places the palla in her hand
- **Typical duration:** 30 minutes
- **Participants:** Bride, groom, raagis, family members who stand around the path
- **Items:** Palla linking bride and groom, cleared walkway around the Guru Granth Sahib
- **Significance:** Four hymns composed by Guru Ram Das are sung; the couple, holding the palla, circle the scripture once per hymn.

## Ardas and Karah Prashad

- **Key:** ardas_karah_prashad
- **When:** End of the Anand Karaj
- **Typical duration:** 20 minutes
- **Participants:** Couple, sangat, granthi
- **Items:** Karah Prashad (sweet offering), serving bowls, langar arrangements
- **Significance:** A closing prayer and a reading (Hukamnama) bless the couple, and everyone receives Karah Prashad.

## Doli

- **Key:** doli
- **When:** After the wedding lunch, from the bride's side
- **Typical duration:** 45 minutes
- **Participants:** Bride, groom, bride's family
- **Items:** Puffed rice or grain to throw over the shoulder, coins, decorated car
- **Significance:** The bride's emotional farewell; she throws grain behind her, wishing prosperity for her parents' home.

## Paani Vaarna

- **Key:** paani_vaarna
- **When:** On the couple's arrival at the groom's home
- **Typical duration:** 20 minutes
- **Participants:** Couple, groom's mother, groom's family
- **Items:** Glass or pot of water, mustard oil to pour at the threshold, sweets
- **Significance:** The groom's mother circles water over the couple's heads and sips it, welcoming the bride home.
